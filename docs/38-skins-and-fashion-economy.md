# Equipment Skins & Fashion Economy

## Overview

**Skins (스킨)** are appearance-only equipment — hat, mask, cape, and weapon visuals, plus full fashion outfits — worn over your stat gear through the [fashion slots](37-interface-controls-and-recovery.md) or fused directly onto equipment. namu.wiki lists skins as one of the game's top-level systems: the high female-player ratio (an old community estimate put it near 70%) made Trickster a near-community game, and **skin prices rose continuously** as look-building ("룩덕") became a core pursuit.

## What skins are

- Appearance items with no stats of their own: weapon skins, head/mask/cape visuals, and per-slot fashion (upper/lower/outer body, waist, head, face, hand, decorations).
- Dual-purpose MyShop gear blurs the line: many MyShop equipment pieces carry both usable stats **and** desirable looks, so a skin purchase can double as leveling gear.

## How skins were acquired

| Source | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| **MyShop equipment tab** | Head/mask/cape/weapon categories; click-to-preview before buying |
| **MyShop fashion tab** | Per-slot pieces or full sets (sets are cheaper than buying individually); preview supported |
| **MyShop random boxes** | e.g. a 20-weapon collection box yielding 1 random weapon skin — chasing one specific skin takes heavy points |
| **Egg Shop crafting-skin boxes** | When a crafting-skin rotation updates, retired skins return inside the "collect-them-all" random box |
| **Events** | Limited-time skins: holiday sets (candy gear, Christmas warm/cold skins), anniversary sets, national-day items (Taegukgi cape/mask), etc. |
| **Player trading** | Personal shops advertising "스킨" in the title, plus 1:1 trades — prices vary wildly, so shop around before buying |

## Applying skins: equipment fusion

Wearing a skin over real gear is [Item Fusion](11-item-fusion.md): the skin item becomes the **image equipment**, your stat gear stays the **stats equipment**, and the fusion consumes an **Artisan's Flame** (장불, community shorthand). Defusing returns a tradable **Form item**, which is exactly how skins circulate in the player economy after their source rotates out.

## The look-building economy

Korean community documentation is blunt about the consequences: skins and decoration items were **the game's top-value trade goods**, inflating for the entire late service; the "play to work" → "play to socialize" shift after the Netmarble era swelled the look-building population; and even hacking stayed marginal because the game's economy made gold farming barely worthwhile — the real wealth was fashion.
